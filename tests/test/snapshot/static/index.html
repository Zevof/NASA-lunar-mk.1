Select Solar System to see all eight rotating 3D planets, Earth’s Moon, and the Sun.
Overview is NOT to scale. Select True scale to see real relative radii and distances, then use Focus selected body.
Sunlight physics: the Sun is so distant that its incoming light direction is effectively parallel at Earth and the Moon. Their shadows can therefore look similarly oriented; visible differences come mainly from each body’s rotation, axial tilt, local terrain and the chosen viewing time.

At 1× terrain relief and True scale, body radii and centre distances are physically proportional. Solar surface granulation is an artistic 3D approximation, not measured solar terrain. Earth terrain uses a lower-resolution NASA-derived topography/bathymetry map. Elevation zero is the lunar reference sphere (radius 1737.4 km). Grid lines follow actual LOLA terrain and connect elevation points to the reference surface. Exaggeration affects only the display. Limited DEM resolution means very small craters may be missing.

Calculation notes

With LOLA data, local horizon obstruction is approximated from terrain samples. Without DEM data, a spherical horizon is used. Contact estimates represent geometric line-of-sight, not received radio power. Earth–Moon flight-path surveys use an Earth-centred two-body approximation; a real mission needs high-fidelity dynamics and trajectory correction. UTC throughout.

Earth → Moon route planner

Choose a departure point on Earth and destination on the Moon. Compare Earth-centred two-body Lambert transfer estimates (3–6 days). The best candidate is ranked by a heuristic combining parking-orbit injection Δv and destination illumination/earth visibility; it is NOT an optimized launch, landing, or safe trajectory.

1. Search Earth departure or click Earth · 2. Choose Moon destination · 3. Pick departure date on the timeline · 4. Choose a route preference · 5. Calculate and compare candidates.
Use the slider to choose a departure time, then calculate a route. Preview-mode results are illustrative.

Trajectory estimates use Lambert's problem with Earth-only gravity and a simplified lunar orientation. In True scale, the light-blue curve uses correctly scaled orbital distances in a Moon-centred moving reference frame; Overview compresses it for visibility. It is not a validated trajectory. Actual launch windows, propulsion, lunar orbit insertion, landing safety, lunar gravity, and communications infrastructure are not solved.

Compare lunar orbital flight paths

Ideal impulsive, coplanar circular-to-circular Hohmann transfer. Three node orientations are compared at the currently selected Earth/Sun snapshot; requires trajectory optimization for a real spacecraft. A triangulated fix can supply a departure position, but not the necessary orbital velocity.

Choose altitudes and compare candidate paths.

Flight paths are ideal Keplerian transfer arcs, without lunar mascons, finite burns, spacecraft limits, ground-station schedules, actual epoch propagation or rendezvous targeting. An orientation scored here may not be reachable at the requested epoch.
